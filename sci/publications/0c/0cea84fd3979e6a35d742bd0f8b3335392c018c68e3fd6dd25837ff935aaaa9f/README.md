# SF6 and CFC-12 transit-time ages followed the 1997-2021 oxygen changes on sigma0 26.5 near Bermuda, but did not explain the 2012-2021 loss at 52 W; age noise prevents attribution off California

## Summary

Two earlier campaign publications found oxygen loss on the sigma0 = 26.5 kg/m3 density surface that solubility and vertical displacement do not explain. Off southern California (CalCOFI), the 1984-2006 decline stopped, and oxygen stayed about 16 umol/kg low in 2007-2025 [P1]. At the Bermuda Atlantic Time-series Study (BATS) site, oxygen on the same surface was 10.8 umol/kg lower in 2016-2025 than in 1989-1998 [P2]. In both records, apparent oxygen utilization (AOU) increased. The open question was: did the water become older (less ventilation), or did it use more oxygen at the same age (more respiration, or a different preformed state)?

We used the transient tracers CFC-12 and SF6 in the GLODAPv2.2023 repeat hydrographic sections. With an inverse-Gaussian transit-time distribution (TTD), we calculated the mean age of the water on sigma0 = 26.5 for each section occupation. Then we compared the AOU change between occupations with a "ventilation term", which is the oxygen utilization rate (OUR) multiplied by the age change. The OUR is not known exactly. Thus we used a lower and an upper estimate (see Methods). Five repeat sections had tracer data on this surface:

- P02/P03 east end, next to the offshore CalCOFI grid (1985, 1994, 2004, 2005, 2013);
- P02 interior, 30 N, 160-127 W (1994, 2004, 2013);
- P16N at 152 W, 25-35 N (1991, 2006, 2015);
- A22 at 66 W, 26-36 N, through the BATS region (1997, 2003, 2012, 2021);
- A20 at 52 W, 26-36 N, eastern Sargasso Sea (1997, 2003, 2012, 2021).

Main results (sigma0 = 26.5; changes are later minus earlier occupation, in umol/kg and years; 95% bootstrap intervals over matched cells):

1. **The sections reproduce the oxygen changes in the time series.** Off California, oxygen fell by 20.4 (11.9 to 30.9) between 1985 and 2005 at the same stations, and by 24.3 (11.6 to 35.2) between 1994 and 2004. The CalCOFI offshore annual anomalies for the same years changed by -24.1 and -20.5. At A22, oxygen changed by -8.9, +11.3 and -18.3 between 1997, 2003, 2012 and 2021. The BATS annual means for the same years changed by -8.5, +9.0 and -12.8. At A20 (52 W) the changes were -11.4, +7.9 and -15.6. Thus the BATS signal is not a local trend. It is a gyre-scale swing: low in 2003, high in 2012, low in 2021.
2. **Near Bermuda (A22), the age changes had the same sign as the AOU changes in all three intervals.** The mean age changed by +2.5 (1.6 to 3.5), -3.5 (-5.7 to -1.5) and +5.4 (3.4 to 7.5) years, while AOU changed by +9.0, -12.6 and +21.0. With the upper OUR estimate (4.5 umol/kg per year), the ventilation term was +11.2, -15.9 and +24.2. The residuals (AOU change minus ventilation term) were -2.1 (-4.8 to 0.0), +3.2 (-5.7 to 12.5) and -3.2 (-12.4 to 5.4). For 1997 to 2021, AOU increased by 18.0 (14.2 to 21.7), the age increased by 4.5 (3.4 to 5.7) years, and the residual was -2.4 (-5.3 to 0.4). Thus a change in ventilation age alone can explain the A22 oxygen changes, but only if the OUR is near its upper estimate. With the lower OUR estimate (0.9), the ventilation term explains about one quarter of the AOU change.
3. **At 52 W (A20), the 2012-2021 AOU increase of 17.8 (16.0 to 19.9) occurred without an age increase.** The age change was -0.3 (-2.9 to 2.2) years. Even with the upper OUR, the residual was +19.3 (8.5 to 30.9). For 1997 to 2021 the residual was +6.3 (3.4 to 9.4). Ventilation age cannot explain this part of the oxygen loss. A change in the source-water mix, in preformed oxygen, or in the utilization rate must contribute. On both Atlantic lines, the water on sigma0 = 26.5 in 2021 was colder and fresher than in 2012 (theta -0.47 and -0.44 degC, salinity -0.15 and -0.14), and the surface was 98 and 177 m deeper.
4. **In the subtropical North Pacific at 152 W (P16N), the 1991-2006 AOU increase of 11.8 (8.0 to 15.8) matched the upper ventilation term (11.7; residual +0.1, -5.6 to 5.3).** The 2006-2015 increase of 7.9 was larger than the ventilation term (residual +3.3, 0.0 to 6.7). For 1991-2015 the residual was +7.5 (3.6 to 11.6). Thus age changes explain part, but not all, of the 24-year AOU increase there.
5. **Off California, the ages do not resolve the cause.** Between the 2004 and 2005 occupations, one year apart and at the same distances from San Diego, the mean age changed by +14.2 (5.3 to 24.5) years, but AOU changed by only +2.4. This age difference in one year is larger than all decadal changes. The cross-shore gradient is large (ages of 60-75 years near the coast in California Undercurrent water and about 40 years offshore). Thus the CFC-12 ages in this box cannot separate ventilation from respiration for the CalCOFI oxygen step. Along the P02 interior, the 1994-2004 AOU increase (20.3, 13.9 to 26.5) was within the range of the ventilation term (residual with upper OUR +7.1, -1.0 to 13.8). In 2004-2013 the age increased (5.1 years) more than the AOU change (4.3) can support (residual -16.1, -20.8 to -11.0).
6. **Method checks.** Paired SF6 and CFC-12 data give a TTD width ratio (Delta/Gamma) of about 1.0 to 1.35, which supports the assumed value of 1. A steady flow with a true ratio of 1.25-1.5 would give a false CFC-12 age increase of 1.7 to 4.6 years between the periods. A 10% decrease in surface CFC-12 saturation would give a false age increase of 7.6 to 13.2 years. In young Sargasso water after 2003, CFC-12 often cannot give an age, because the atmospheric CFC-12 concentration stopped increasing (7 of 17 A22 casts in 2012 had no CFC-12 age). Thus the SF6 ages carry the Atlantic 2012-2021 result.

7. **The classification is the same on sigma0 = 26.4 and 26.6.** At A22, all four pairs agree with ventilation on all three surfaces. At A20 (2012-2021) and at P16N (1991-2015), AOU increased more than the upper ventilation term on all three surfaces.

Interpretation: on the decadal scale, oxygen on sigma0 = 26.5 near Bermuda varied with the ventilation age of the water, which agrees with changes in mode-water renewal. The long-term BATS loss came with older water at 66 W. But at 52 W in 2012-2021, and in the North Pacific at 152 W over 1991-2015, AOU increased more than the age change can explain, even with the upper OUR. For the CalCOFI oxygen plateau, the available tracer sections are too noisy to answer the question. These conclusions depend on an OUR between two bounds that differ by a factor of 2 to 10. A better OUR constraint (for example from tritium-helium ages or from a model) is the main need.

## Question and plan

Question: are the isopycnal oxygen losses at CalCOFI and BATS caused by older water (ventilation) or by a higher oxygen use at the same age (respiration, preformed state, or source water)?

Prediction for ventilation only: AOU change = OUR x (mean age change), so the residual is near zero and the AOU change has the same sign as the age change. A residual that stays positive with the upper OUR shows that ventilation age cannot explain the AOU increase.

We defined the boxes, the sections and the 26.5 surface before we calculated the age changes. We saw the occupation-mean oxygen values (not the ages) before we chose the sections. We changed two choices after a first run: (1) cell matching by distance from San Diego in the CCS box (the first run used 1 x 1 degree cells, which matched only 1-3 cells because the tracks differ in direction); (2) the "SF6 else CFC-12" age, because CFC-12 cannot give an age for 7 of 17 A22 casts in 2012. We also report the CFC-12-only result for each pair (`files/results/pairs.csv`, columns `*_cfc12`).

## Methods

1. **Boxes.** CCS: 28-35 N, 127-117 W. NPSTG: 25-35 N, 160-127 W. SARG: 26-36 N, 72-56 W (A22 passes 2 degrees west of BATS). SARGE: 26-36 N, 56-46 W.
2. **Isopycnal values.** For each cast and each variable, we interpolated linearly in sigma0 between the two flag-2 bottles that bracket the surface. The bottles had to be within 0.30 kg/m3 and 250 m of each other. We did not extrapolate. Surfaces: 26.3, 26.4, 26.5, 26.6 and 26.7.
3. **TTD ages.** The tracer partial pressure is the convolution of an inverse-Gaussian TTD with the northern-hemisphere atmospheric history, times a saturation factor. We inverted for the mean age Gamma with Delta/Gamma = 1 and 100% saturation. Grid: Gamma 0.25-600 years; integration step 0.02 years; the tracer histories are zero before 1910. We used the decreasing branch of the predicted partial pressure against Gamma. Values above the attainable maximum (very young water after the atmospheric CFC-12 maximum) have no age. Sensitivity ages: Delta/Gamma = 0.5 and 1.5, 90% saturation, CFC-11, and SF6.
4. **Primary age.** SF6 age where the cast has SF6, else CFC-12 age.
5. **Matching.** We matched occupations by 1 x 1 degree cells. In the CCS box, the tracks start at San Diego but have different directions, so we matched by 100 km bins of distance from San Diego. A cell value is the mean of the casts of one occupation in the cell. A change is the mean over the matched cells of (later - earlier).
6. **OUR bounds.** OUR_fe is the least-squares slope of AOU on age across the cells of each occupation, with occupation fixed effects, pooled over the occupations of a section. Errors in the ages bias this slope toward zero, so we use it as a lower estimate. OUR_bulk is the sum of AOU divided by the sum of age over all cell-occupations of a section. If the preformed AOU is not negative and the OUR is constant along the path, the true OUR is not more than OUR_bulk. We use it as the upper estimate. Values on sigma0 = 26.5 (OUR_fe / OUR_bulk, umol/kg per year): CCS 1.55 / 3.70; P02 interior 3.14 / 4.02; P16N 1.87 / 3.72; A22 0.92 / 4.52; A20 0.49 / 4.73.
7. **Intervals.** We resampled the matched cells (1,000 draws) and, independently, the cells that give the OUR estimates. We report the 2.5% and 97.5% quantiles. Seed 20261110.
8. **TTD checks.** (a) For casts with both SF6 and CFC-12, we found the width ratio at which the two ages agree (ratios 0.2-2.5). (b) For each box, we took the early-period cell ages as the true ages under steady flow, calculated the partial pressures in the early and late years with a different true width ratio or saturation, and inverted them with the standard settings.
9. **Box period contrasts (secondary).** `files/results/contrasts.csv` compares fixed periods (CCS and NPSTG: 1984-1995 against 2003-2014; SARG and SARGE: 1988-1999 against 2010-2021) with 2 x 2 degree cells and all cruises in each box. The SARG and SARGE late period mixes the high-oxygen 2012 and low-oxygen 2021 occupations, so these contrasts hide the swing.

## Limitations

- **OUR.** The attribution depends on the OUR, which we bracket but do not measure. The two bounds differ by a factor of 2 (North Pacific) to 10 (Sargasso). The upper bound assumes a constant OUR along the flow path and a preformed AOU that is not negative. If the OUR is higher in young water, the effect of an age change on AOU can be different from our linear term.
- **TTD assumptions.** We assume one inverse-Gaussian TTD with Delta/Gamma = 1, steady surface saturation and the northern-hemisphere atmosphere. Our checks show that changes in saturation can give false CFC-12 age changes of several years. Mixed SF6/CFC-12 ages assume that both tracers see the same TTD.
- **Few occupations and few cells.** Each section has 3-5 occupations. The matched cells are 3-22 per pair, and nearby cells are not independent, so the intervals are probably too narrow. The cell bootstrap does not include calibration errors between cruises (GLODAP states 5% for the transient tracers and 1% for oxygen).
- **Seasons.** Occupations are in different seasons (for example, CCS 2004 in June and 2005 in November). Seasonal changes in the California Undercurrent can change the water on sigma0 = 26.5.
- **Space.** A22 is about 2 degrees west of BATS. The CCS box covers the offshore part of the CalCOFI grid, not the core grid of [P1].
- **No model comparison.** We did not compare with ocean models. This part of the original question stays open.
- **Multiple tests.** We report many pairs and checks without correction for multiple tests.

## Reproduction

`files/code/run_all.sh SCRATCH OUT [CAMPAIGN_DIR]` downloads the sources into SCRATCH (or reads the shared copies with `USE_SHARED=1`), extracts the boxes, calculates the ages, runs all analyses and draws the figures. Record-level intermediates stay in SCRATCH. Steps:

- `fetch_data.sh`: download and SHA-256 check.
- `extract_glodap.py`: box selection and isopycnal interpolation.
- `ttd.py`, `ages.py`: TTD convolution and inversion.
- `analyze.py`: box period contrasts, OUR fits, TTD shape and saturation checks, SF6/CFC-12 width ratios.
- `analyze_pairs.py`: repeat-section pair decomposition (primary result), for sigma0 = 26.5, 26.4 and 26.6.
- `consistency.py`: comparison with the CalCOFI and BATS series.
- `summarize.py`: classification of each pair against the ventilation bracket on three surfaces.
- `figures.py`: figures.

Software: Python 3.12.16, numpy 2.5.4, pandas 3.1.1, matplotlib 3.11.2, pyarrow 26.0.1, geopandas (map land layer only). The machine had a very high load during the work; one run of `analyze_pairs.py` took about 30 minutes for this reason, not because of the computation.

Aggregate results in `files/results/`: `pairs.csv` (primary), `pairs_sig26.4.csv`, `pairs_sig26.6.csv`, `sections*.csv` (OUR bounds), `occupations_pairs*.csv` (occupation means), `occupations.csv`, `contrasts.csv`, `our_fits.csv`, `shape_bias.csv`, `sf6_ratio.csv`, `timeseries_consistency.csv`, `bracket_summary.csv`, `results.json`.
